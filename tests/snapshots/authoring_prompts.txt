You turn a business policy, written for people, into rules a solver can check. The whole document is below, every sentence numbered, under its heading.

## Delivery refunds policy
1. What customers get when a parcel is late, lost or damaged.

## Late deliveries
2. An express parcel more than 2 days late gets a refund if the customer asks.
3. Express customers get a $10 voucher for any delay.
4. A customer never gets both a refund and a voucher.

## Couriers
5. Courier parcels are never refunded.
6. A late courier parcel is refunded if the customer asks.
7. Lost parcels are always refunded.

## Damaged and sale items
8. Damaged items are always replaced.
9. Sale items can't be returned or exchanged.

## Refund requests
10. Refunds for late delivery are only given when the customer asks.

How to write the policy:
- sorts: a choice between named values, e.g. {"name": "Carrier", "values": ["POST", "COURIER"]}.
- variables: every fact a rule uses. Each has a name (letters and digits, camelCase), a sort (Bool, Int, Real or a sort's name), a role and a description of what it means, literally.
  - role "system": the business's own systems know it (orders, tracking, payments). Never asked.
  - role "ask": only the customer can say. Give the question the agent asks, in plain words.
  - role "derived": decided by the rules, e.g. whether the customer gets a refund.
  - display: how an author reads each value. For Bool and choice variables, a phrase per value ({"true": "Asked for a refund", "false": "Didn't ask for a refund"}); for numbers, text with one {} ("{} days late").
- rules: each cites the number of the sentence it comes from. A sentence may give several rules.
  - kind "when": when `when` holds, `then` holds. Most sentences are this.
  - kind "define": `var` holds exactly when `as` holds. Only when the sentence really says "only if" or "means".
  - kind "constraint": a fact about the world that always holds, e.g. days late is never negative.
- Expressions: a variable's name; true/false, numbers, or a choice value's name; {"not": e}; {"and": [e, ...]}; {"or": [e, ...]}; {"implies": [a, b]}; {"iff": [a, b]}; {"eq": [a, b]} and likewise ne, lt, le, gt, ge; {"add": [...]}, {"sub": [a, b]}, {"mul": [...]}; {"in": [e, [value, ...]]}; reals as {"real": "2.5"}.
- outcomes: what a customer can end up with, each a name, an assignment of derived variables to values, and a display ("A refund"). Give an outcome for every result the document describes, including getting nothing.
- Only state what a sentence says. Don't infer a fact the document doesn't state.
- A sentence that doesn't decide what a customer gets (a promise about timing, a greeting, background) is background: extract nothing from it.
